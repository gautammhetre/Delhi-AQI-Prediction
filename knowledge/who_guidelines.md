## What the WHO guidelines are
The World Health Organization published updated Air Quality Guidelines in September 2021, the first update since 2005. They are health-based recommendations, not legal limits. Most guideline values were tightened because evidence showed harm at lower levels than previously thought.

## WHO guideline values for particulate matter
PM2.5: 5 µg/m³ as an annual mean and 15 µg/m³ as a 24-hour mean. PM10: 15 µg/m³ as an annual mean and 45 µg/m³ as a 24-hour mean. The PM2.5 annual guideline is half the previous value of 10 µg/m³.

## WHO guideline values for gases
Nitrogen dioxide: 10 µg/m³ annual mean, 25 µg/m³ 24-hour mean (the annual value is four times lower than before). Ozone: 100 µg/m³ as an 8-hour mean, and 60 µg/m³ as a peak-season mean. Sulphur dioxide: 40 µg/m³ as a 24-hour mean. Carbon monoxide: 4 mg/m³ as a 24-hour mean.

## How Delhi compares with the WHO guideline
India's own NAQI calls PM2.5 up to 60 µg/m³ "Satisfactory", which is four times the WHO 24-hour guideline of 15 µg/m³. In the dataset used by this app, Delhi's average PM2.5 over 2020–2023 was about 238 µg/m³, roughly 16 times the WHO 24-hour guideline.
