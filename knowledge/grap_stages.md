## What GRAP is
The Graded Response Action Plan is a set of emergency measures for Delhi and the National Capital Region, run by the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM). Measures become stricter as the AQI rises, and each stage keeps the measures of the stages below it. CAQM invokes a stage based on the current AQI and forecasts, so a high AQI on one reading does not by itself mean a stage has been declared.

## GRAP Stage I – Poor air quality (AQI 201 to 300)
Measures include stopping construction and demolition on larger sites that are not registered for dust monitoring, enforcing vehicle pollution checks, action against visibly polluting vehicles and industries, and public awareness drives.

## GRAP Stage II – Very Poor air quality (AQI 301 to 400)
Adds a ban on coal and firewood in hotels, restaurants and eateries, restrictions on diesel generator sets except for emergency and essential services, higher parking fees to discourage private vehicles, and more frequent public transport services.

## GRAP Stage III – Severe air quality (AQI 401 to 450)
Adds a wider ban on construction and demolition except essential and specified projects, closure of stone crushers, brick kilns and mining, closure of industries that use unapproved fuels, and possible restrictions on older BS III petrol and BS IV diesel cars. The December 2024 revision moved some measures that were earlier in Stages III and IV into lower stages.

## GRAP Stage IV – Severe+ air quality (AQI above 450)
Adds a ban on entry of most trucks into Delhi except those carrying essential goods or running on clean fuels, a ban on most diesel vehicles except BS VI and essential services, and closure of polluting industries. State governments may also move schools to online classes, allow 50% work from home in offices, and consider odd-even vehicle rules.
