## Who is most vulnerable to air pollution
The health ministry lists these groups as most at risk: children under five and older adults, pregnant women, people with existing lung or heart disease, people in poor living conditions or with poor nutrition, and outdoor workers such as traffic police, construction workers and street vendors.

## Outdoor exercise, walking and jogging on polluted days
When air quality is poor to severe, avoid outdoor physical exertion. The advisory specifically says to avoid outdoor walks, runs, jogging and exercise in the early morning and late evening, and to reschedule outdoor activities based on the AQI. This applies to children's outdoor play too.

## Opening windows and ventilating the home
Keep external doors and windows closed during the early morning and late evening, when pollution is usually highest. If the home needs ventilation, do it in the afternoon, roughly between 12 noon and 4 pm.

## Keeping indoor air cleaner
Use wet mopping instead of sweeping, use a vacuum cleaner with a HEPA filter if needed, and cook with clean fuels such as gas or electricity instead of wood, coal or other biomass.

## Masks and N95 respirators
The advisory treats masks as an optional choice. A disposable N95 or N99 respirator can help if it fits properly, the instructions are followed and exposure is short. It should be replaced after use. Paper masks, handkerchiefs, scarves and cloth masks are not effective against fine particles.

## Things to avoid burning
Avoid burning firecrackers, wood, crop residue, leaves or other biomass, and avoid smoking cigarettes and bidis, which add to both indoor and outdoor pollution.

## Symptoms to watch and when to see a doctor
See a doctor if you have breathlessness, dizziness, cough, chest discomfort or pain, or red or watery eyes. People in vulnerable groups should seek medical advice immediately if their symptoms get worse.
