## What the National Air Quality Index is
India's National Air Quality Index (NAQI) was launched by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB). It turns the concentrations of up to eight pollutants (PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, NH3 and lead) into one number on a 0 to 500 scale and one of six colour-coded categories, so the public can understand air quality at a glance.

## How the AQI number is calculated
Each pollutant gets its own sub-index from its concentration, using breakpoint bands. The overall AQI is the worst (highest) sub-index, and that pollutant is called the prominent or responsible pollutant. A valid AQI needs data for at least three pollutants, and one of them must be PM2.5 or PM10. Particulates, NO2, SO2 and NH3 use 24-hour averages; CO and ozone use 8-hour averages.

## Good (AQI 0 to 50)
Air quality is good and the health impact is minimal.

## Satisfactory (AQI 51 to 100)
Sensitive people may feel minor breathing discomfort.

## Moderate (AQI 101 to 200)
People with lung disease such as asthma, people with heart disease, children and older adults may feel breathing discomfort.

## Poor (AQI 201 to 300)
Most people may feel breathing discomfort if exposed for a long time. People with heart disease may feel discomfort.

## Very Poor (AQI 301 to 400)
Long exposure may cause respiratory illness. The effect is stronger in people with lung and heart disease.

## Severe (AQI 401 to 500)
Even healthy people may have respiratory effects, and people with lung or heart disease can be seriously affected. Health effects may be felt even during light physical activity.
